🏭 Precisio CNC 5-Axis Machining Centre Controller

Manufacturer: Precisio Steuerungstechnik AG  ·  Classification: Important Class I  ·  First placed on market: July 2024

An industrial 5-axis CNC machining centre controller with integrated PC-based HMI, real-time motion controller, and networked production management interface. Supports STEP-NC and G-code. Connects to factory MES/ERP via OPC-UA and MQTT. Remote diagnostics via encrypted VPN tunnel. Used in automotive, aerospace, and medical device manufacturing.

What makes this product complex

  • OPC-UA server — a rich, standards-based industrial protocol with a large attack surface. Any unauthenticated exposure is a significant finding.
  • Remote VPN access — a remote data processing element under Article 3(2). CRANE's classification wizard determines it is in scope (I1: manufacturer-developed; I3: necessary for remote diagnostics; I5: direct product interaction; I6: bidirectional data).
  • Important Class I — requires either a harmonised standard or third-party conformity assessment before CE marking.
  • Industrial sector overlap — the Machinery Regulation and NIS2 both apply to the deployment context, requiring careful boundary-drawing.

How CRANE manages it

SBOM & vulnerability scanning

CycloneDX SBOM uploaded per firmware release. Trivy and OSV scan surfaces CVEs in the embedded Linux stack, OPC-UA libraries, and MQTT client. EPSS scores prioritise the OPC-UA NodeId enumeration finding (CVE-2024-38876, CVSS 6.5) over lower-risk dependency issues.

PSIRT workflow

Claroty Research Team report received via CVD policy. CRANE logs the report, tracks the 90-day remediation timeline, links the fix to security update v1.0.1, and publishes advisory SA-2025-001. Audit trail records every state transition with timestamps.

Release gate

Gate for v1.0.0 required: approved STRIDE risk assessment, passed SBOM validation, Notified Body certificate, no open critical CVEs. Gate for v1.0.1 (security patch) required: VEX closure on CVE-2024-38876, updated SBOM, re-validated gate items.

Article 3(2) scope decision

VPN diagnostic backend assessed via I1/I3/I5/I6 wizard. All four criteria met → classified as CRA Art. 3(2) in scope. Rationale documented and linked to the product record. Notified Body can review the decision directly in the platform.

🏢 NexHub Building Management Sensor Hub Gen3

Manufacturer: NexConnected Systems SRL  ·  Classification: Default  ·  First placed on market: March 2025

An IoT gateway and sensor hub for smart building management systems. Aggregates data from up to 256 sensors (temperature, humidity, CO₂, occupancy, energy metering) via Zigbee 3.0, Z-Wave, and Modbus RTU. Connects to building management software via REST API and MQTT over TLS. Supports OTA firmware updates. Deployed in commercial buildings, hospitals, and data centres.

What makes this product complex

  • Large component footprint — embedded Linux, Zigbee/Z-Wave stacks, MQTT broker, REST API server, OTA update client. Each layer carries its own CVE exposure.
  • Frequent OTA updates — quarterly firmware releases mean ongoing SBOM management and vulnerability re-triage.
  • Default class — self-assessment is sufficient, but the evidence standard must still be met. CRANE's Annex I matrix keeps track of which requirements are covered and which have gaps.
  • Cloud backend scope — the NexCloud management portal is assessed via the Article 3(2) wizard. It provides optional remote management; local operation is possible without it → classified as out of scope (I3 criterion not met).

How CRANE manages it

SBOM quality management

Initial SBOM for v3.0.0 scored 62/100 — missing supplier names for several Zigbee stack components. CRANE's quality report identified the gaps. v3.1.0 SBOM scored 88/100 after remediation. NTIA minimum elements now pass.

Vulnerability triage

OSV scan of v3.0.0 surfaces 14 CVEs in embedded dependencies. EPSS filtering identifies 2 with >10% exploitation probability. VEX assessments close 11 as not-affected (vulnerable code path unreachable in the build configuration). 1 patched in v3.1.0. 2 accepted with risk justification.

CVD policy & incoming reports

CVD policy published via CRANE and linked from product documentation. Two external reports received: one from a penetration tester (REST API authentication bypass — patched in v3.1.0), one false positive (closed with VEX justification).

Support period tracking

5-year support period declared for Gen3 (2025–2030). CRANE monitors end-of-support date and triggers alerts at 1 year, 6 months, and 3 months before expiry — giving the product team time to plan an extension or end-of-life notification.

🚆 RailSecure ETCS Level 2 Onboard Unit

Manufacturer: RailSecure GmbH  ·  Classification: Important Class II  ·  First placed on market: January 2025

A European Train Control System (ETCS) Level 2 onboard unit for main-line railway operations. Implements Subset-026 v3.6.0 baseline movement authority, speed supervision, and cab signalling. Communicates with Radio Block Centres via GSM-R and LTE-R. Safety-certified to EN 50128 SIL4. Deployed on high-speed and conventional lines across four EU member states.

What makes this product complex

  • Important Class II — third-party Notified Body assessment is mandatory. No self-assessment route exists for this classification. The evidence standard is the highest in the CRA.
  • Sector overlap (ERA Regulation) — railway signalling is also governed by the European Union Agency for Railways (ERA) under the TSI CCS. The CRA and ERA frameworks must be reconciled; CRANE records both sets of obligations.
  • EN 50128 SIL4 safety certification — every change must pass through a joint safety and cybersecurity assessment. CRANE's substantial-change module tracks whether a modification triggers both a new safety case and a new CRA conformity assessment.
  • Long support commitments — railway onboard units have operational lifespans of 20–30 years. Support period obligations under CRA Article 13 are significant.
  • Air-gapped GSM-R interface — the radio communication backend is a third-party operator (rail infrastructure manager). Assessed via the Article 3(2) wizard: I1 criterion not met (not manufacturer-developed) → classified as third-party component, not in CRA scope for the manufacturer.

How CRANE manages it

Notified Body evidence package

Gate for v1.0.0 includes: TARA risk assessment (approved), SIL4 safety certificate, SBOM with CRA Phase 2 validation, penetration test report, hardware security architecture document, and Annex I coverage map. All assembled as a single export from CRANE for the Notified Body submission.

Substantial change tracking

v1.1.0 introduced a new LTE-R radio module replacing the original GSM-R hardware. CRANE's substantial-change assessment: new radio interface → new attack surface → assessed as substantial. Triggers full re-conformity assessment and a new Notified Body review cycle. Documented rationale stored in version history.

Long-term support period

20-year support period declared (2025–2045). CRANE tracks the obligation across the full period with configurable alerts. End-of-support notifications planned for operators at 2-year, 1-year, and 6-month thresholds before the declared end date.

TARA risk assessment

TARA methodology selected (automotive/railway standard). Threat scenarios cover: RBC spoofing via radio, unauthorised movement authority injection, denial-of-service on the communication channel, and physical tamper of the onboard unit. Each scenario has documented impact, likelihood, and mitigation measures with approval workflow.

Managing the landscape together

What makes CRANE valuable isn't just managing a single product — it's having all three of these products, their releases, vulnerabilities, and obligations in one auditable platform. The dashboard surfaces the items that need attention across the whole portfolio:

Portfolio-wide vulnerability view

A single view of all open CVEs across all products, sorted by EPSS score. The CNC controller's OPC-UA CVE and the IoT hub's MQTT broker patch appear alongside each other — letting the security team prioritise across the whole product range, not just per product.

Upcoming end-of-support alerts

The lifecycle alerts module monitors all three support periods simultaneously. As the NexHub Gen3 approaches its 2030 end-of-support date, alerts fire at the configured thresholds — across all product lines in the same notification stream.

Annex I coverage gaps

The Annex I matrix per product shows which essential requirements still have open evidence gaps. The portfolio view makes it clear which products are furthest from a complete evidence package — helping prioritise compliance work before an assessment deadline.

Audit trail across all products

Every action across all three products is recorded in the same immutable audit log. A single export covers the complete activity history of the whole product portfolio — exactly what a Notified Body or internal compliance team needs for a portfolio-level review.
